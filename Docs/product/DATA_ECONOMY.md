# Data economy and enterprise marketplace

## Purpose

WRS may create commercial value from high-quality data that participants intentionally
contribute and authorize. The marketplace is a controlled licensing and delivery
system, not a public file store and not a blanket right to commercialize member data.

## Dataset categories

- voice and speech;
- African, global, local, and indigenous languages;
- text, translation, transcription, handwriting, and documents;
- image, video, gesture, movement, navigation, and robotics;
- agriculture and other industry workflows;
- healthcare, legal, and finance only under specialist, jurisdiction-aware programs;
- facial data only with explicit, granular consent and heightened review.

Categories describe content. Each project separately defines purpose, source,
population, geography, consent, retention, sensitivity, quality rubric, and permitted
uses.

## End-to-end lifecycle

1. **Project definition:** customer or WRS defines purpose, instructions, prohibited
   content, target population, quality threshold, rights, and compensation policy.
2. **Consent and eligibility:** contributor sees project-specific use, recipients,
   retention, withdrawal limits, and reward terms before accepting.
3. **Collection:** task assignment, source metadata, client version, and consent version
   are attached to every submission.
4. **Review:** automated, human, and specialist checks create an immutable review trail.
5. **Curation:** transformations, deduplication, redaction, sampling, and exclusions are
   recorded as lineage.
6. **Release:** authorized approvers sign a versioned manifest with quality, privacy,
   rights, safety, and consent coverage.
7. **Licensing and delivery:** an enterprise contract defines permitted use, access,
   security, retention, onward transfer, deletion, and incident duties.
8. **Monitoring and closure:** usage, renewals, revocation obligations, complaints,
   security incidents, and distributions remain linked to the released version.

## Dataset registry

Every release candidate has:

- dataset and version ID, title, owner, status, and sensitivity class;
- item count and modalities, languages, geography, collection period, and known limits;
- provenance and transformation lineage;
- consent coverage and excluded/withdrawn item count;
- review rubric/version, validator coverage, quality metrics, and known bias findings;
- permitted/prohibited uses, license version, retention, and deletion procedure;
- release approvals, customers, deliveries, incidents, and deprecation status.

Statuses are `Draft`, `Collecting`, `Reviewing`, `Release candidate`, `Approved`,
`Licensed`, `Suspended`, `Deprecated`, or `Deleted`. An approved contribution is not
automatically an approved dataset.

## Enterprise experience

Potential customers include AI and robotics companies, universities, research
institutes, automotive and language-technology teams, and qualified healthcare or
agriculture technology organizations.

The enterprise surface supports requirements intake, catalog discovery, a data sheet,
sample access where allowed, contract workflow, secure delivery, access logs, usage
attestation, support, and incident reporting. Sensitive datasets use a gated workspace
or controlled computation rather than direct download when appropriate.

## Participant distributions

A project may define a distribution program. It must publish:

- the qualifying commercial event and covered dataset version;
- gross receipts or another defined base, deductions, platform fees, and pool amount;
- contribution, quality, rarity/demand, consent, and validation factors;
- minimum thresholds, caps, timing, currency, tax treatment, reversals, and disputes;
- whether package entitlement affects only access/limits or a disclosed participation
  factor.

Quality and accepted contribution must remain primary. Package purchase alone never
creates a distribution. The UI labels values as estimated, pending, or confirmed and
must not display forecasts as account balances.

## Non-negotiable controls

- no collection before an active purpose-specific consent or other documented lawful
  basis approved for the program;
- no facial/voice/movement reuse under a generic analytics checkbox;
- no licensing without provenance, rights, release approval, and customer terms;
- no validator may approve their own work or their own release without separation;
- withdrawals/deletions propagate according to the consent and contract policy, with
  any legal/technical limitation explained before contribution;
- prohibited content, minors, sensitive traits, surveillance, and high-risk uses require
  explicit program policy and legal/safety review.

## Phasing

- **MVP:** task catalog, consent, uploads, automated checks, staff moderation, internal
  dataset registry, no open enterprise marketplace.
- **Phase 2:** human validation, lineage, release candidates, controlled buyer pilots.
- **Phase 3:** expert review, enterprise catalog, contracting, delivery, licensing,
  distributions, safety monitoring, and release governance.
