# WRS delivery roadmap

The architecture should anticipate the full ecosystem, while product delivery follows
evidence and operational readiness. Dates are intentionally omitted; phase exit gates
matter more than calendar promises.

## MVP — foundation

### Outcome

A member can safely create and grow a digital robot profile through learning and basic
data contributions, with every reward and financial status clearly explained.

### Scope

- account, verification, session security, robot activation, and passport;
- package entitlements framed as platform access rather than investment;
- training modules and structured data tasks;
- automated file/schema/quality checks plus staff/community moderation;
- XP, badges, event codes, and a separated wallet/read model;
- opportunity discovery and simulated/digital deployment states;
- community announcements, events, support, consent, deletion, and audit records;
- admin tools for tasks, moderation, events, rewards, and incidents.

### Exit gates

- no unresolved critical consent, deletion, auth, ledger, or reward-abuse defects;
- users distinguish XP, credits, pending money, and confirmed money in testing;
- contribution provenance is complete from task assignment to disposition;
- staff can review, reject, appeal, suspend, and audit without database edits;
- package and earnings language passes legal/compliance review for launch markets.

## Phase 2 — trusted network

### Outcome

WRS can scale contribution quality and community support without central staff reviewing
every action.

### Scope

- Community and Professional Validators (Levels 1–2);
- calibration, review assignment, consensus, appeals, reviewer quality, and trust scores;
- contributor quality score and advanced-task eligibility;
- Ambassador and City Leader dashboards, events, onboarding, and support queues;
- role applications, training, credentials, terms, review, suspension, and removal;
- dataset registry, lineage, consent coverage, and internal release candidates;
- verified digital service opportunities and reconciliation.

### Exit gates

- validator agreement, gold-set accuracy, and appeal metrics meet approved thresholds;
- no leader can see member data outside the minimum geographic/program scope;
- role granting and revocation are fully audited and independent of package purchase;
- quality score changes are explainable, versioned, and appealable;
- at least one real buyer pilot has defined requirements before public marketplace claims.

## Phase 3 — enterprise network

### Outcome

WRS can deliver traceable, consented datasets and specialist review to contracted
enterprise customers.

### Scope

- Expert and Master Validators (Levels 3–4) with credential verification;
- specialized medical, legal, finance, engineering, and agriculture review programs;
- controlled dataset catalog, buyer workspaces, contracts, licensing, delivery, and
  revocation/incident procedures;
- contributor distribution programs with transparent, program-specific rules;
- AI safety reviews and formal Validator, Technical, Ethics, and Community councils;
- enterprise analytics and immutable release manifests.

### Exit gates

- customer contract, permitted-use enforcement, and release approval are operational;
- every released item has traceable consent, source, transformations, reviews, and
  license terms;
- specialist programs have jurisdiction-aware credential and liability policies;
- marketplace revenue, fees, pool allocation, and participant distribution reconcile;
- external privacy/security review is complete for the relevant dataset class.

## Future — physical robotics

Physical deployment is a separate operating capability, not an automatic extension of
the digital profile. It requires real inventory/device identity, custody, control,
maintenance, safety telemetry, insurance, incident response, customer contracts, and
clear ownership terms.

## Explicit non-goals for MVP

- open enterprise dataset downloads;
- automated medical, legal, or financial expert approval;
- country, regional, or global titles without active operational need;
- physical robot ownership or guaranteed deployment;
- cash conversion of XP, points, badges, or boosts;
- compensation based primarily on recruitment or package purchase.
